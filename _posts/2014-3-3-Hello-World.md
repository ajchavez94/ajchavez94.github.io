# About Me

Hi! I’m **Andrea Janine Chávez Pacheco**, but you can call me Andre or Andreita.  
I’m a passionate learner and explorer of the world, always eager to discover the wonders of nature and the stories hidden within landscapes, forests, and people.

I am currently in my second year of my **PhD in Bioscience engineering**, working between the **QForestLab at Ghent University in Belgium** and the **Escuela Politécnica Nacional (EPN) in Ecuador** as part of the **ReCoAndes project**, supported by the Belgian cooperation program **VLIR-UOS**.  
My research focuses on how tropical montane forests respond to climate change, combining field monitoring, stable isotope analysis, and community collaboration.

I grew up surrounded by Ecuador's diverse ecosystems, which shaped my deep connection to biodiversity and my curiosity for environmental issues. Over the years, I’ve been involved in various projects, ranging from chasing monkeys in the Amazon to long-term forest monitoring of mountain regions, botanical expeditions, and regional science networks. These experiences constantly remind me of the value of collaboration and the richness that comes from blending different perspectives, disciplines, and cultures.

Beyond science, I am also an **illustrator** and visual storyteller.  
I enjoy translating complex ideas into accessible drawings that spark curiosity and conversation.  
I’ve contributed to projects like the **Women in Science Ecuador** calendar, educational games, and outreach materials that bridge gaps between scientific knowledge and local communities.

This blog is a small window into my journey — a place where I collect notes, field stories, reflections, sketches, and moments that shape my life as a researcher and as a person.  I write in both **English and Spanish**, depending on the audience and the mood. So, feel free to explore, comment, and join me as I navigate the Andes, attend international conferences, sketch new species, or pause to reflect on what it means to do science in a rapidly changing world.

When I’m not measuring leaf traits or analyzing data, you’ll probably find me drawing plants, cooking, or planningg  my next hike in the mountains.

**Thank you for stopping by — I hope you find something here that inspires you, too.**
